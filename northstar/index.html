ComboSchoolA proposal for North Star Jazz Workshops · Fall 2026

Schedule and messages, one system

The schedule talks.

Every rehearsal, jam and concert comes with its conversation. When something on the schedule changes, the right people hear about it in their messages, and every message takes them straight back to the date.

Members post their own gigs, say I'm going to the jams, and find their section in #sax or #bass. And when it's time to practice, every tune your group plays opens to its changes in your own key, written out as real notation, with a rhythm section ready to play along.

And I built all of it in one day.

It's running now: northstar.jazzcombo.org

By Curt Bergmann · alto sax, Group 12

How this happened

One member. One day. All of this.

I wrote the first line of ComboSchool at 10:20 on a Monday morning. By that evening it was running on the open internet at its own address, with North Star's schedule, groups, jams and tunes in it, a user manual for every role, and hundreds of automated tests checking it every time anything changed. Software that used to take a team months now takes a determined person a day. I still can't quite believe it, so here's the receipt.

The big idea

Your calendar and your group chat, finally talking.

Most schools run the schedule in one place and the conversation in another: a calendar, an email list, a group text. ComboSchool is one system, so the two stay in step on their own.

One app instead of two. North Star already uses My Music Staff (linked from its website) and the free version of Slack, which shows only the last 90 days of messages and deletes anything older than a year. ComboSchool could replace both: schedule, lessons, rosters and every conversation in one place, with history kept for good. Billing and payments are on the roadmap; until then, My Music Staff can keep handling those.

  • Every group has its conversation, right beside its schedule. Group 12's chat and its Sunday rehearsals live together.
  • Changes reach exactly the right people. A sub needed, a coach out, a jam published: the people involved hear it in Messages, and by email if they chose.
  • Every notice opens the date. Tap Open and you're on the rehearsal, with the answer buttons right there.
  • Recent shows it all together: the sub request, the reply about it, and the office's announcement, newest first.

Talking to each other

Your whole group, or just one person.

Every group gets its own conversation the day its roster is set: charts, tempos, who's bringing what. Nobody has to collect phone numbers or start a group text.

Members message each other directly, one to one or a few at a time, without trading personal numbers. A bass player and a drummer can book Saturday's practice room in three messages.

  • A group's conversation includes exactly its current roster, plus guardians of any minors.
  • A direct message that includes someone under 18 automatically includes their guardians.
  • Unread counts, Recent, and email for the messages you choose.

For coaches: subs

The coach decides. The app does the asking.

A player who can't make Sunday just says so. Chris gets a notice and picks what happens: no sub, ask a few people (players of the same instrument come up first), or open it to anyone.

ComboSchool sends the asks, the first yes gets the spot, and Chris, the player and the group are all told. If a sub backs out, the same people are asked again; if Chris asked the wrong person, Un-ask sends an apology. No phone tree.

Nobody finds out at the door anymore.

Every step is a message to exactly the people involved, in the app and by email, the way each person chose.

Channels

A listening club, right inside.

Beyond the groups, anyone can join topic channels, the way Slack works. This week #listening-club is on Blue Train: Coltrane, Lee Morgan and Curtis Fuller, recorded in 1957.

  • Browse every channel and join or leave with one tap.
  • A topic line anyone in the channel can change: "Now listening: Blue Train".
  • Bold, italics and links in any message. A link gets a preview card, like the album cover here.
  • Open channels for everyone, or private ones by invitation. Guardians come along with minors.

Section channels

Every section has its own room.

#sax, #trumpet, #trombone, #bass, #keys, #guitar, #drums, #vibes, #vocals: ComboSchool makes a channel for every section from the instruments members play. Nobody has to set them up.

  • You're already in yours. Alto, tenor and bari players all land in one #sax, and the channel follows your profile when you add an instrument.
  • Open to anyone. A pianist curious about voicings can join #guitar, and leave with one tap.
  • Instruments are picked, never typed, from a searchable list with a Main one first. So a sub request reaches the right players, and every part comes out in the right key.

Jams & events

Drafted, reviewed, then announced.

A new jam, clinic, show or concert night starts as a draft that only staff and faculty can see. Faculty mark Looks good or leave a note.

When it's ready, the office taps Publish and announce, and everyone gets the message with its own announcement text. Members see a New badge until they've seen it.

Anything marked public also goes up on North Star's public calendar, open to anyone with no sign-in and laid out like the events page on northstarjazzworkshops.com: the next date up top, then the rest of the season.

Gigs

Where everyone's playing this week.

Members play all over town, and until now you heard about it by word of mouth. The Gigs tab is where they tell each other. Anyone taps Post a gig at the top: the day, the room, the lineup, the cover.

Mine is on there: the Take Your Time Quartet, third Thursdays, 1 to 3:30, at Showtime Studio & Coffee, with John Pohl, Jon Norstad and Maria Meade.

  • Everyone sees it, with a New badge, and posting it announces it to the whole school in Announcements, with an Open link.
  • I'm coming puts it on your Home page and your schedule, and tells the band how many to expect.
  • The poster edits or deletes it; the office can remove any gig.

On your phone

Say you're going. It's on your Home.

The Second Sunday Jam at Dual Citizen Brewing, the Metronome Jam on the last Tuesday at MetroNOME Brewery, the Showtime Jam on the first Wednesday: North Star's jams, member concerts, clinics and faculty nights sit together under Jams & Events, and anything you haven't seen is marked New.

Answer I'm going, Maybe or Can't make it to a jam, clinic or show, I'm coming to a concert, and it joins Coming up on your Home page beside your rehearsals and the gigs you're going to. Guardians see their kids' too.

  • Rehearsals set once as a series: 2nd and 4th Sundays, 2:30 to 4:30, Jazz Central basement.
  • Double-booked rooms are flagged for the office.
  • Phone apps for iPhone and Android come from the same code.

Coach cover

Out sick? One button tells the faculty.

A coach taps I can't make it, find cover. Faculty see it at once, a colleague takes it or the office assigns one, and the group hears who's leading. The sub can take attendance for that one rehearsal and nothing more.

Between rehearsals

Then go home and practice.

The schedule gets everyone into the room. The tune pages make the room sound better: every tune your group plays, with its chord changes in your own key, practice exercises written out as music, and a bass player and comper who never get tired of the bridge.

Tunes: the changes

Every chart, in your key.

Open a tune and its chord changes are right there: a clean grid, four bars to a row, with section letters, real repeat signs, first and second endings, codas, segnos and the final barline. Written for your instrument, not just "E♭ or B♭": alto and tenor, trumpet, clarinet, trombone in bass clef, guitar an octave up, voice. 43 instruments in all, and Keys for switches to anyone else's.

The changes come from a 3,337-chart chord reference (iReal Pro playlists, the iReal forum and the Real Book). The library starts with 666 standards; search for anything else, and a tune with changes joins your practice list or your group's book with one click.

Practice views

Real notation, the way you read.

One tap turns the changes into engraved music, in your key and your clef, following the tune's form bar for bar. Five ways into the same tune:

Roots
Each chord's root, held as long as the chord lasts. Hear the bass line, learn the form.
Arpeggios
The chord tones, up and down, through every change.
Scales
The scale for each chord, stepping through the changes, with the altered notes in view.
Leading tones
One connected line of chord tones, 3rds and 7ths first. From the 3rd and From the 7th give two paths.
Analysis
A lead sheet of the harmony. Simplified names the scale in force; Advanced adds Roman numerals, ii–V–I brackets and secondary dominants.

It's the same analysis and the same exercises as my own improv practice app, held to that app's output character for character by automated tests.

Backing track

A rhythm section on call.

Press Play and a band comes in: sampled bass, Rhodes comping and a woodblock click. The groove comes from the chart, or pick one: Swing, Latin, Ballad, Waltz, Jazz Manouche.

Jazz Manouche brings in a guitar playing la pompe the way Django's rhythm players did, ka-BOOM, CHAK, and it keeps pumping through bars with two chords.

  • Your tempo, a bar of count-in, and a loop that goes round as long as you do.
  • Your mix: Bass, Chords and Click each have a volume, and in a practice view the Line plays the exercise over the band.
  • Never lose your place. The bar being played lights up in the grid, in the notation and on the Analysis lead sheet, through repeats and endings as written.
  • Stop is always in reach, pinned to the bottom of the screen however far you scroll.

For the whole group

Everyone on the same page, in their own key.

A group's repertoire lists its book, and under each tune a Parts line gives every player's key from the instrument they play in that group. Nobody shows up to rehearsal having learned it in the wrong key.

  • Players build the book. Anyone in an ensemble can add tunes to its repertoire; classes, jams and events stay with their instructors.
  • And Sunday's list. Players add tunes to their group's upcoming rehearsals and can take off the ones they added; once the rehearsal is over, only the instructor changes it.
  • My practice list, private to each player (and a guardian for minors), sits at the top of Tunes.
  • Concert nights carry each group's set, so the program and the practice pages are the same tunes.

Safe by design

  • Members under 13 have no login; guardians act for them.
  • Messages to a minor include their guardians.
  • Lessons are private to teacher, student and guardians.
  • Sign in with an emailed link or a passkey (Face ID or Touch ID), no passwords. The sign-in page remembers your email; untick it on a public computer.
  • The rules are enforced in the database itself.

Coming next · not built yet

Invoices and payments, with Stripe.

The last thing My Music Staff does that ComboSchool doesn't: monthly invoices and paying online. The plan is to add it with Stripe, the standard for apps like this.

  • The money goes straight to North Star. The school connects its own Stripe account. ComboSchool never holds funds and takes no cut.
  • Monthly invoices built from rosters and lessons, with a Pay online button and automatic "Paid" once the payment clears.
  • Autopay. A member or guardian saves a card or bank account once on Stripe's secure page and ticks Pay automatically each month. Reminder before each charge, emailed receipt after, automatic retries if a card fails.
  • Members stay in control: switch autopay off, change the card, or pay any invoice by hand.
  • The office sees it all: who's on autopay, what's paid, what's overdue.

From the builder

I went a little crazy today.

I retired from a career in technology, and for a while now I've been building two apps for myself: one to run my gigs, and one to practice jazz improvisation. Today I went a little crazy and built this for North Star: a website that works on any phone, with iPhone and Android apps next, all in a day.

I mean that literally. Everything in this brochure, every screen you see, the messaging, the schedule, the gigs, the channels, the tune library and the practice tools, went from nothing to running live between a Monday morning and that evening, with 618 automated tests and three user manuals to show for it. Along the way I fixed things in my own two apps, because they share the same music engine. Five years ago this would have been a team and a budget. Today it was me, a laptop, and a very long cup of coffee.

Honestly, it helps me too. As a Group 12 player I wanted one place for our schedule, our conversation and our tunes. The tune library, the chord charts and the practice views come straight from my own apps, so the tools that help me learn a tune are here for everyone.

That's the part I'm proudest of. The harmonic analysis, the exercises and the backing band are the same ones I practice with every day, checked against my app note for note. If a student at North Star wants to know why the bridge of Autumn Leaves feels the way it does, it's one tap away, in their key.

Curt Bergmann · alto sax, Group 12

The ask

One term. Three or four groups. Free.

  1. Pick the pilot groups and willing coaches. Group 12 is already set up.
  2. The office invites those members on the live site. Each signs in from an emailed link, then saves a passkey if they like.
  3. Coaches run subs, cover and group messages through it; the office posts those groups' jams and concerts; players RSVP, post their gigs and learn the book from the tune pages, in their own keys.
  4. At term's end, compare time spent on subs and cover, and ask members whether they'd keep it, and whether they practiced more.

Questions for the faculty

  • Which groups should pilot, and which coaches are in?
  • Should subs default to "ask people" or "open to anyone"?
  • Who publishes events, and do drafts need faculty sign-off?
  • Is email enough for last-minute changes until the phone apps arrive?
  • Would coaches point players to the practice views between rehearsals, or use them in the room?
  • What would make you stop using it?
